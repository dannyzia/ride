## (b) Findings demanding HIGH-ALTITUDE structural responses, not line edits

**B-1. Severity: Blocker** (Stage 5 Finding 1 — confirmed live in code; the fix is architectural, not a bullet).
Affected Scope: §3.5 "Accept-time snapshot" (L290), Item 13 (L465–468), M-14 invariant restatement (L292), tests 39/40.
Verified in the working tree: the accept path hard-rejects any driver with no `call_ledger` deduction row (`no_deduction` return). The design's own hazard-hunt exterminates this invariant *class* elsewhere (orphan sweep, repair fallthrough, blind sum) but leaves its one executable instance untouched. The fix cannot be a one-line edit: it must redefine accept-proof per mode (commission acceptance proved by the live `dispatch_offers` row instead of the deduction row), which reshapes the M-14 invariant restatement, Item 13, and the invariant suites. Treat as a §3.5 sub-section rewrite covering all accept-path consumers, not an Item 13 append.

**B-2. Severity: Major** (supersedes Critic A-3; now has a verified mechanism-level sibling).
Affected Scope: §3.5 kill-switch (L279–290), Item 12 (L460–463), Item 13 (L465–468), §3.9 rollback (L342); verified `utils-server/dispatch.ts:183-185`, `leadBilling.ts:60,105`, `index.ts:2890,2970,2976,3078`.
The kill-switch's layering is undecided, and the Items contradict each other at the mechanism level: Item 12 "retires the dispatch-level `revenueModel` parameter from the per-driver path" while Item 13 says "kill-switch read remains for OFF-mode legacy path" — but the parameter has verified consumers beyond the per-driver debit: the auto-accept early-return (`index.ts:2976`), the `lead_cost_calls` payload (`:3078`), and leadBilling's own `commissionOnly` gate (`leadBilling.ts:105`). If the parameter is retired, OFF mode cannot run legacy leadBilling; if OFF-mode leadBilling is conditional on the parameter, the parameter is not retired. Combined with Critic A-3's frozen/enrolled rollback hazard, the structural decision required is: enumerate which layers are kill-switch-gated (pool-entry predicate, per-row classification, auto-accept gate, payload derivation) and pick ONE OFF-mode story (unconditional possession predicates + gated classification, or parameter-retained-legacy-path). Every layer must be named; "byte-for-byte legacy" cannot survive an unconditional classification rewrite.

**B-3. Severity: Major** (Critic A-4 + A-5, generalized).
Affected Scope: §3.5 L288–290; G1 (L45).
The two G1 windows share one structural root: the ride's monetization mode is evaluated independently at offer time (classification) and accept time (snapshot), with no durable binding between them and an unlocked read at the second evaluation. The high-altitude fix is to bind the mode ONCE, durably, inside the classification transaction (stamp the snapshot there, or carry the offer's classification onto the accept stamp in the accept tx) — and lock the enrollment lookup in the classification tx. Line edits that merely add `FOR UPDATE` close one window while leaving the mode-of-record doubly-derived.

**B-4. Severity: Major** (rollout architecture; generalizes Critic A-8).
Affected Scope: §3.9 Stage 3 (L341), §3.6 L298, Items 16/17/24.
Stage 3 asserts "client + API together," which EAS/OTA and store-review distribution cannot deliver — propagation takes days to weeks. The server-side changes are immediate for ALL callers, old and new. The structural response is a client-compatibility policy: a client-version floor or a config-gated parallel legacy purchase path, plus deprecated stubs for removed/redefined dues fields. This is a rollout-architecture decision (config flag vs version header vs route versioning), not a field-rename edit. See D-1 for the purchase-side consequence no stage caught.

**B-5. Severity: Major** (comprehension F-6 + F-7, plus the PURCHASE_BLOCKED family from Stage 5 F-2 / Critic A-10 — shared root neither stage named).

Aging, the no-product counter, and server-side `PURCHASE_BLOCKED` all fail for the same architectural reason: the design persists **no lifecycle events** — lockouts are info-level log lines, switches are row mutations without history, blocks are request-scoped 409s. ## (c) NEW architectural-level weakness revealed by the findings taken together — named by neither stage

**Severity: Major.**
Affected Scope: G1 (L45), Item 14 reconciliation job (L473), Item 40 drift-guard test (L610–613), §3.8 observability (L334).
Stage 5 found the accept guard rejects commission drivers; the Critic found two G1 windows (accept re-derivation, unlocked enrollment read). Read together they expose what neither said: **G1 — "exactly one of {lead debit, commission accrual} per completed ride" — has no enforcement or reconciliation at the ride grain anywhere in the design.** Exclusivity is enforced only incidentally at two moments (debit-at-offer in leadBilling; snapshot-gating at completion), and the design's durability net does not cover it: Item 14's reconciliation audits subscription↔bill pairing, Item 40's drift-guard audits the owed-figure across three read paths, and §3.4's "invariant test" (L334) covers only one direction (commission accrued while snapshot = subscription). Nothing anywhere asserts the converse — that a completed ride with snapshot = `'subscription'` actually produced a deduction pair, or that a snapshot = `'commission'` ride produced accrued commission (or a 0% basis). Consequence: the Critic's windows are not merely open, they are **silent** — a ride that falls through either window (neither billed nor accrued, or both) leaves zero drift signal in the entire observability and reconciliation plan. The zoom-out-level fix: extend Item 14's alert-only job (or add a completion-time runtime assertion) to carry G1 per completed post-cutover ride. This converts G1 from a restated AGENTS.md prose invariant (Item 34) into an auditable one, and it is the natural completion of fixing B-3.

## (d) Remaining weeds / dead-ends the earlier stages missed

**D-1. Severity: Major.**
Affected Scope: §3.4 (L259–263), §3.9 Stage 3 (L341), G6/G8; Item 24.
(New — old-client purchase window.) Every stage audited the dues-endpoint compatibility (Critic A-8) and missed the bigger one: the rewritten purchase route **removes invoice initiation for all callers** ("No PortPos invoice is initiated at purchase," L259; response keeps `payment_url: null`, L263). An un-updated client in the EAS window that taps "Buy ৳400 package" receives granted calls plus an open bill with **no checkout and no dues itemization to see it** — a driver unknowingly carrying invisible debt until their client updates. Money-safe (the receivable is recorded; a retry 409s on the narrowed gate) but support-hostile, and it contradicts the document's own zero-disruption posture (G6/G8). The rollback direction is symmetric: after a kill-switch revert, old clients still cannot render subscription bills. This is the concrete payload of structural finding B-4; at minimum Stage 3 needs a stated old-client purchase policy.

**D-2. Severity: Minor.**
Affected Scope: §3.4 cross-mode dust paragraph (L273), §3.6 dues payload (L298), `MIN_PAYMENT_BDT` (verified `dues/pay+api.ts:33`).
(New — stranded dust becomes a dead pay target.) The dust rule is analyzed only for blocking. Consider the actual cross-mode sequence the paragraph itself describes: commission driver carries < ৳10 dust, switches to subscription (settle-first passes), buys a package. That dust can now **never be settled** — it is below the ৳10 settlement minimum, and the driver accrues no further commission (snapshot = subscription zeroes completion). It renders forever on the itemized dues screen as a commission line whose only pay control always fails the minimum — a standing Copy Truth violation (UI offering an action the backend always rejects). Non-blocking by D1, so the trap claim in L273 is technically true but the display claim is false. Fix is small but must be specified: suppress commission dues below the settlement floor from the pay path (and/or from display), or permit dust to piggyback on a bill settlement.

**D-3. Severity: Minor.**
Affected Scope: Item 12 (L460–463) vs Item 13 (L465–468); verified `index.ts:2976,3078`, `leadBilling.ts:105`.

The implementation brief downstream of this document would resolve the contradiction by whichever reading the implementer lands on — exactly the drift class the design exists to eliminate.
