# Postpaid (Credit) Monetization with Per-Driver Product Choice

* **Author:** Elephant (EGM brainstorm, six-lens Goldfish synthesis), for Zia
* **Date:** 2026-11-07

## Overview

Ride becomes fully postpaid toward its drivers. Two purchasable products: the existing call packages (subscription) and a new ৳0 commission package. The product a driver holds IS their monetization mode — no mode column with a default; every driver must hold one of the two to receive offers; new drivers choose at onboarding. Subscription bills are generated immediately at purchase and calls are granted BEFORE money arrives (credit). Commission bills are generated as completed trips. One unpaid-obligations figure per driver feeds three enforcement mechanisms: settle-first at purchase, ceiling-based dispatch lockout, and manual payment via the dues screen. Subscribers accrue zero commission; commission drivers are never billed for subscription.

**Reviewer status:** all six lenses completed — design_critic, codebase_details, zoom_out, comprehension (after one rate-limit retry per protocol), implementation_readiness, structural.

**Verdict on the original question ("is this possible, how hard is it"):** Possible. Every needed primitive already exists in the codebase — revenueModel-aware lead billing (`utils-server/leadBilling.ts:54-110`), the full commission-debt/settlement machinery (`lib/commissionDues.ts`, `lib/paymentRepair.settleCommissionDues`), a proven pay-later invoice pattern (`purpose='commission_settlement'`), a proven activate-without-gateway pattern (the ৳0 trial path, `app/api/package/purchase+api.ts:323-338`), per-driver advisory locks, and the Idempotency-Key convention. But this is NOT a configuration change. It inverts the payment activation flow that five files jointly enforce today, and six of the findings are Blockers that each require a structural decision before any design document can be written. Honest effort shape: additive schema is small; the cost concentrates in (1) the purchase/activation/callback/compensation rewrite, (2) per-driver classification inside dispatch and lead billing, (3) a single-source unpaid-total function plus its drift guards, (4) the observability layer (lockout events, exposure metrics — none of the current lockout machinery is visible in production logs today), and (5) a substantial driver-facing UI surface (onboarding choice, pause banners, itemized dues, redesigned purchase confirmation). Difficulty: moderate-to-high, dominated by the money machinery and the split-process rollout, not by the schema.

## Analysis

### Blockers (must be resolved in the design; each is a consequence of the owner rulings, not an attack on them)

**B1 — The ৳500 ceiling locks out every package buyer on day zero.** The lockout ceiling defaults to ৳500 (`lib/platformConfig.ts:269`, enforced at `utils-server/dispatch.ts:530-531`). Under postpaid, a freshly granted package is a receivable equal to its full price at the moment of purchase. If any live package price is at or above the ceiling, the buyer is dispatch-locked at activation. Related dust-due deadlock: settlement enforces a minimum payment of ৳10 (`app/api/driver/dues/pay+api.ts:33,115-120`), so a driver with a sub-৳10 remainder can be permanently barred — settle-first blocks purchase, the only settlement path rejects the amount, nothing writes off. **Required:** fix ceiling semantics before anything else — count only overdue bills past an age threshold (recommended; survives future price changes), or enforce ceiling ≥ max live package price + headroom via a seed/migration check, or per-product ceilings; plus an explicit dust-dues rule (writeoff threshold, waiver, or allowance).

**B2 — Grant and bill must be one transaction; the repair path inverts with it.** Today `lib/activateSubscription.ts` runs subscription insert + `initial_load` ledger row + event `paid` in one transaction under a per-driver advisory lock (`activateSubscription.ts:8-58, 32`), only after `verifyIPN` (`app/api/payment/portpos/callback+api.ts:58-65`). Postpaid grants calls at purchase initiation. If the grant and the unpaid-bill row are not written in one transaction, a crash between them yields granted calls with no receivable — a silent revenue leak. `repairPaymentEvent`'s driver_package branch (`lib/paymentRepair.ts:242-243` → `activateSubscription`) currently repairs by marking paid; its semantics must be re-specified to "grant + open bill", and every caller (callback, compensation worker) inherits that change. **Required:** atomic grant+bill in one tx (reusing the advisory lock); repair path re-specification; a reconciliation invariant — every active subscription has exactly one bill row — checked by a scheduler job (the repo already has the orphan-paid pattern, `src/db/schema.ts:1075-1077`).

**B3 — The payment_events machinery cannot represent "money owed, not yet received"; reusing it corrupts state.** `paymentStatusEnum` is only `initiated | paid | failed | callback_pending` (`src/db/schema.ts:104-109`). Two legacy consumers make overloading unsafe: the orphan sweep auto-enqueues any `callback_pending` event with no subscription older than 10 minutes (`utils-server/scheduler.ts:1169-1197`), and `repairPaymentEvent`'s fallthrough calls `activateSubscription` on any unpaid driver_package event (`lib/paymentRepair.ts:207-243`) — which either throws `amount_mismatch` and doom-loops, or (if amounts match) runs the supersede sequence that EXPIRES a paying driver's validly granted subscription mid-retry. Separately, the callback short-circuits any event that already carries a `subscription_id` (`callback+api.ts:114-119`) — so if the grant stamps the purchase event, the later settlement payment on that same event is unreachable: money arrives and nothing clears. And `lib/commissionDues.ts:48-54` plus the dispatch mirror (`dispatch.ts:302-311`) sum ALL completed settlements with no purpose discrimination — routing subscription settlements through the same table would subtract subscription money from commission dues. **Required:** purchase-grant and settlement are SEPARATE payment_events with separate purposes (new purpose value, e.g. `subscription_settlement`; the TS purpose unions in `lib/paymentEvents.ts:31,70` must be extended); unpaid obligations live in a representation the legacy sweep/repair cannot touch; every settlement row carries a kind discriminator from day one; compensation enqueues only for failed settlements, never for unpaid bills.

**B4 — Settle-first is racy without a per-driver serialization.** Two concurrent purchases (fresh client Idempotency-Keys are valid — keys are per-claim, not per-driver) can both read "no dues" before either bill commits: two grants, two receivables. This race exists in prepaid form today (the `active_subscription_exists` gate at `purchase+api.ts:276-294` is transaction-free; double collection is already possible via the supersede path). **Required:** a partial unique index — at most one OPEN bill per driver — plus the settle-first check and bill creation inside one transaction under the existing per-driver advisory lock pattern (`activateSubscription.ts:32`), acquired at initiation time; the 23505 barrier handled idiomatically as the codebase already does (`lib/idempotency.ts:150-166`).

**B5 — Dispatch-level revenueModel classification silently double-bills a mixed pool.** `debitLeadForOffer` receives `revenueModel` as a once-per-dispatch constant (`utils-server/index.ts:2890` → every debit). Under possession=mode one chain mixes subscriber and commission drivers; a pool-level constant bills commission drivers one lead per offer. Also, ranking: commission holders (and any driver with `calls_remaining = null`) get `balanceScore = 0.0` (`dispatch.ts:628-632`) and are weighted last — in-mode drivers systematically deprioritized. And a commission package modeled as a ৳0 subscription with `call_count = -1` would rank top as "unlimited" — the −1 sentinel must not be reused to mean "no lead billing"; branch on product type. **Required:** per-driver mode classification INSIDE the debit transaction (the `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` at `leadBilling.ts:83-84` already serializes this); the dispatch-level parameter demoted to a default/kill-switch; balanceScore and the pool filters become per-row predicates keyed on product type with commission holders given a fixed neutral rank; per-driver economics in the offer payload (`lead_cost_calls`, `index.ts:3076-3078`).

**B6 — The v6 shadow fare engine hard-codes commission 0 and becomes authoritative at Stage 1.** `complete+api.ts:192-194` comment, hard zero at `:261`, and the stage switch at `:275` (`authoritative = stage1 ? v6FareBreakdown : fare`). The moment commission-mode goes live while the fare framework's Stage 1 is also on, every commission bill silently becomes ৳0 — no error, no log, no test failure. **Required:** v6 takes commission percent from the same possession-aware source as v2; the hard-code and its comment are deleted as part of this change, not deferred; invariant test: commission-holder + percent > 0 + Stage 1 on → authoritative commission > 0 and equals the v2 figure.

**B7 — "What does this driver owe" must have ONE definition, structurally enforced.** The repo already carries the documented hazard: `lib/commissionDues.ts:12-13` warns "keep the two definitions of net dues in step" between the lib function and the hand-mirrored dispatch sums — a rule that lives only in a comment. Postpaid adds a third leg (unpaid subscription bills) and a fourth consumer (the account-deletion gate, `lib/accountDelete.ts:59-66`). Drift is bidirectional damage: stale-low = free leads for indebted drivers; stale-high = paying drivers locked out. Also the existing commission-debt sums are an unbounded all-time aggregate over lifetime completed rides on the dispatch hot path (`dispatch.ts:291-300`) — extending that pattern scales linearly forever. **Required:** one authoritative expression in the database (a view, e.g. `driver_unpaid_obligations`) that unions unpaid bills and the accrued-minus-settled commission math; the per-driver function, the batched dispatch query (`GROUP BY driver_id` over the view, O(open bills) not O(lifetime rides)), the purchase gate, the dues screen, and the deletion gate all select from it; a drift-guard test asserting batched sum equals canonical function on the same fixture — the executable replacement for the comment.

**B8 — Every lockout mechanic the concept builds on is invisible in production today.** The logger defaults to info level (`lib/logger.ts:1-3`); both per-driver filter logs are debug-level (`dispatch.ts:533-537, 559-561`) and therefore not emitted; the only info-level signal is an aggregate that names no drivers and does not run under the launch-default model. There is no persisted lockout event, no reason code, no enumeration of locked drivers; `is_dispatch_locked` is recomputed transiently per read. Under postpaid, FOUR silent gates can zero a driver's offer flow, plus settle-first purchase blocks. **Required:** per-driver lockout/unlock events with typed reason `{no_product, subscription_lapsed, balance_exhausted, debt_ceiling, settle_first_blocked}`, exposure amount, and ceiling, persisted or info-level; an admin surface enumerating locked drivers with reason and aging; outcome logging on every silent `billed=false` exit in `leadBilling.ts` (five exist today, none logged); an accrual event per ride (driver, ride, percent, basis, commission) with an anomaly alert for "commission accrued on a subscriber" and a logged zeroing event when mutual zeroing suppresses an accrual.

**B9 — The driver-facing experience of the gates is undefined; silence is the failure mode.** The concept defines server gates but zero driver states. A driver who is online and receiving no offers — for any of four reasons — will conclude the app is broken, not that they owe money. The only existing lockout surface is a card on the dues screen (`due-amounts/index.tsx:346-368`), reachable only by navigation and only under the global commission-gated model. And the day-zero purchase inversion has no UX contract: today tapping "Buy" opens a PortPos checkout (`packages.tsx:118-119`); postpaid grants calls with no payment prompt, which drivers trained by the current app will read as a failed purchase or a free product. **Required:** an explicit driver-state machine (ACTIVE / OWING / CEILING-LOCKED / LAPSED / PURCHASE-BLOCKED / PRODUCT-FROZEN), surfaced on the home screen with a one-tap fix per state; a real-time "you are paused — reason + fix" banner driven by a state push or poll with distinct copy per reason; a post-purchase confirmation showing "N calls added · bill ৳X — pay now or pay later"; the word "free" forbidden (Copy Truth Rule); settle-first block errors deep-link to the dues screen with pay pre-loaded, ≤ 2 taps from block to payment initiated.

### Major findings

**M-1 — Shipped prior art exists and must be ruled on: the courier COD-dues/hold system.** `lib/deliverySettlement.ts` already implements overdue-days + threshold dispatch hold (`courierDebtHoldsDispatch`, `:145-201`, config keys `lib/platformConfig.ts:232-236`), a remittance-dues ledger with shortfall evidence, pool filter (`lib/deliveryDispatch.ts:163`), and an accept-time guard (`app/api/delivery/offers/[id]/respond+api.ts:52`). The concept must cite this as prior art and rule reuse-vs-divergence explicitly, or a naive implementer duplicates it with divergent semantics.

**M-2 — The missed code map.** The concept undercounts the toggle's blast radius: production `getRevenueModel()` call sites are 3 (`dispatch.ts:183`, `utils-server/index.ts:2890`, `app/api/driver/dues+api.ts:40`), with ~10 consumption points — including the admin config validation surface (`app/api/admin/config+api.ts:191,241`), the offer-card `lead_cost_calls` derivation, and the live-check harness `scripts/check-driver-dues-live.ts` (which flips `revenue_model` and asserts the dues response shape the new model changes). Missed readers needing rulings: admin dashboard platform revenue (sums ride commission only — under postpaid, platform revenue splits across two lanes and this view silently reports one), fleet finance, driver commission-statement/trips/earnings surfaces, `calculate-price`, and the account-deletion gate. Missed facts: `call_ledger` `expiry_writeoff` is a DEAD enum value (no writer exists — lapse is a silent 60-second scheduler flip with no ledger row), and the dispatch pool admits expired subscriptions (left join checks `status='active'` only, not `expires_at`), so lapsed subscribers burn offer slots between expiry and the scheduler flip. The pool possession predicate must include `expires_at > now()`, matching the debit-time check.

**M-3 — Possession must derive from immutable per-driver snapshots, never live package rows.** Admin actions on `packages` (deactivate, soft delete — packages are soft-deletable) would silently flip or orphan live drivers' modes; a vanished package row = "holds neither" = fleet-wide lockout. **Required:** mode derives from state written at activation (a product-type snapshot on the subscription/enrollment row); admin package edits affect future purchases only. Completion must likewise use a mode snapshot taken at offer/accept time (the repo's own precedent: the MFS settlement snapshot taken at trip start, `complete+api.ts:430-435`); completion never re-derives mode from live possession, which also resolves the switch-mid-ride ambiguity.

**M-4 — A2′ (frozen calls) contradicts an existing invariant and needs either new machinery or a simpler owner decision.** A driver holds exactly ONE active subscription (`subs_one_active_per_driver`, `schema.ts:658-660`), and every activation runs the supersede sequence that EXPIRES the prior active row (`activateSubscription.ts:32-35`) — "frozen until switch-back" has no storage representation (`subscriptionStatusEnum` is `active|expired|suspended`). Options: an additive nullable `frozen_at` column (rollback-lossless; prefer over enum widening, which is one-way) consumed by a single shared predicate in both `dispatch.ts` and `leadBilling.ts`; or the simpler policy — switching lets calls lapse naturally (consistent with hard lockout), with optional credit on switch-back only if the owner rules a rollover policy exists. Owner decision required; the onboarding choice screen cannot honestly say "you can switch later" until this is decided.

**M-5 — The trial/launch-free flow conflicts with "no default, choose at onboarding."** Today new drivers are auto-granted a ৳0 trial with zero choice (`purchase+api.ts:296-338`, `ensureLaunchFreePackage`). The trial must map onto exactly one product (which one? does it occupy the one-open-receivable slot? does it consume the one-trial guard, `schema.ts:661-663`?), the onboarding-choice failure state needs definition, and a "registered driver with no possession" counter is needed — the silently-dead fleet state.

**M-6 — The dues API and screen are commission-only shaped; postpaid breaks both.** `GET /api/driver/dues` returns the GLOBAL `revenue_model` and aliases `total_outstanding_bdt` to commission dues only (`dues+api.ts:38-64`) — under per-driver modes this is wrong twice, and a subscriber owing a subscription bill has no line item and no pay button anywhere. **Required:** itemized dues payload (subscription bills, commission bills, total — one function, four consumers per B7), the global revenue_model field removed or redefined, the dues screen restructured per line item, and the settle-first gate + screen computing from the same single source (the exact drift class `lib/commissionDues.ts` exists to kill).

**M-7 — Payment timing semantics do not exist yet.** No due date, no grace period, no dunning: a driver who never buys again and never opens Dues accrues debt invisibly until ceiling lockout — the harshest possible first notification. **Required:** bill due-date semantics (even "due only at next purchase"), at least one proactive reminder before lockout (warning threshold, e.g. 50% of ceiling), purchase screen showing credit headroom before purchase ("this purchase uses ৳400 of your ৳500 limit"), ceiling check before invoice creation with a typed error.

**M-8 — Rollout order and rollback are safety-critical and fully specified by the findings.** If API routes ship per-driver possession while utils-server still runs global gating, a commission-package holder is pooled under `requiresSubscription=true` and dropped with zero offers — or worse, at the debit step with no offer row and no trace. **Required order (matches AGENTS.md deploy order):** (1) schema/migrations first; (2) utils-server second, written so EMPTY possession data degrades exactly to today's behavior (guaranteed, because the old API cannot yet sell commission packages); (3) API/EAS last, enabling sales. Plus a fresh-read platform_config kill-switch consulted once per dispatch and at the purchase route, so behavior rollback is a config write, not a redeploy. Rollback is lossy in three ways unless: open bills stay readable/payable post-revert; granted calls have an explicit policy (let-run-down recommended vs force-expire); settlements stay kind-discriminated so legacy dues math never eats subscription money. The possession lookup must be a join on the pool query, never a per-offer read (zero-queries-inside-tx rule, `leadBilling.ts:44-53`).

**M-9 — Grandfather policy for pre-switch commission accrual.** The completion path has no revenue-model check (`complete+api.ts:289-293` writes commission whenever the pricing percent > 0), so commission accrued BEFORE the switch under the old global-toggle semantics becomes ENFORCING the moment a driver holds a commission package — and settle-first then blocks the very switch that surfaces it. Zero-and-forgive vs keep-counting is a product decision to surface to the owner, not to choose silently.

**M-10 — Schema allow/forbid list (converged across lenses).** ALLOW: `packages.product_type` (`'call_package' | 'commission'`, NOT NULL DEFAULT `'call_package'` — additive, inert to old code) with the purchase rail reused unchanged (the `code` discriminator and live-unique index at `schema.ts:528,559` are purpose-built for this); a commission-enrollment representation with its own one-active-per-driver partial unique index; a bills representation outside the payment_events status vocabulary; nullable `frozen_at` if A2′ survives. FORBID: a `mode` column on `drivers` (second source of truth, contradicts possession); reusing `payment_events.status` for bill state (B3); reusing `driver_commission_settlements` without kind discrimination (B3/F4); `calls_remaining = -1` semantics for "no lead billing" (B5); CHECK constraints or Zod schemas that reject −1 (sentinel rule). Every new money write runs inside `db.transaction` with its event row.

**M-11 — Driver vocabulary and error codes.** The dues screen already mixes three terms for money owed ("COMMISSION DUE", "UNSETTLED", "TOTAL OUTSTANDING" — `due-amounts/index.tsx:298-393`); postpaid makes vocabulary precision a money-clarity issue. Required: one Bengali + one English term each for bill / amount due / pay / locked, applied everywhere with the i18n parity gate. Minimum error-code inventory with human messages: `dues_outstanding_purchase_blocked`, `credit_ceiling_exceeded`, `no_active_product`, `subscription_lapsed`, `payment_in_flight`, plus 403/409/422 mappings. Note: raw codes leak to users today (`onboarding/index.tsx:96` renders `${err.code}: ${err.message}` in an Alert) — new codes must ship with polished messages or they will surface verbatim.

**M-12 — Fraud and exposure numbers.** With settle-first airtight, per-driver exposure is bounded and small (subscribers: one open bill ≤ max package price; commission drivers: ≤ ceiling + a few fares of structural overshoot between ceiling-crossing and settlement — tolerance by design, checked at offer time, accrued at completion). The real exposure is portfolio-level and fraud: account creation is free, so a fraud ring extracts min(package price, ceiling) per account. Required: state the expected-loss-per-fraudulent-account figure in the PRD and extend the existing purchase fraud gate (`checkPackagePurchaseGate`, `purchase+api.ts:52-91`) to commission enrollment and dues-payment retries. Payment-rail QPS does not grow (one PortPos invoice per package as today; settlement reuses the existing purpose pattern); DB writes add one bill row per completed trip — acceptable, and worth stating so reviewers stop re-litigating gateway load.

**M-13 — Transition surface needs named single writers.** Eight implied transitions (onboarding choice; buy subscription → grant+bill; settlement; commission accrual; mutual zeroing; switch preserving calls; lapse/lockout; admin waiver) — three have no writer today (T1 onboarding choice, T5 subscriber zeroing, T8 admin waiver/adjustment), and T6 collides with the supersede sequence (M-4). Required: a transition table mapping each to exactly one writer file, added to the AGENTS.md write-ownership map (mirroring the accepted `lib/fleetAssignment.ts` precedent); possession resolved through ONE function (`getDriverMonetizationMode`) called once per dispatch like `getRevenueModel` today.

**M-14 — Test surface: two pinned invariants invert; eight new invariant tests are mandatory.** Inverting: `dispatch-commission-debt.test.ts:172-184` pins "subscription_only disables the debt gate entirely" (unified ceiling re-gates subscribers); `leadBilling.test.ts:723-777` pins the revenueModel param behavior the kill-switch resolution retires; AGENTS.md dispatch invariant (6) "every offered driver has a call_ledger deduction row" is already false for commission drivers and must be restated per-mode. New mandatory tests: unified-ceiling gating in both modes; settle-first typed 409 + concurrency pair (two simultaneous purchases cannot both pass — advisory-lock pin in the `tests/concurrency` lane); no double-billing — for one ride exactly one of {lead debit, commission accrual}, ideally DB-constrained; mutual zeroing at completion; settlement reduces the unified figure exactly once per event (subscription-bill leg needs its own unique barrier analogous to `dcs_payment_event_idx`); grant↔bill atomicity (crash leaves neither); v6 shadow commission parity; the drift-guard meta test (B7).

### Nits

- The concept's "~7 call sites" figure is wrong (3 call sites / ~10 consumption points — see M-2); `complete+api.ts:289-293` is the wait-fee branch only, the primary percent computation lives in `lib/fareCalc.ts:154`; "all commissions 0% at launch" conflates three storage locations (ride percent in versioned pricing rows; delivery/shop percents in seeded platform_config keys).
- Delete the phrase "fixed bill = 0" — mutual zeroing is expressed by absence of bill rows and a zeroing check, not by a ৳0 bill row (which would also collide with the callback amount-mismatch guard if ever routed to the gateway).
- Settle-first and the ceiling are the same predicate at different thresholds — one shared function, not two gates.
- The trip-complete receipt should render a commission line for commission-mode drivers (first awareness of a charge = the trip that caused it).
- Pull a minimal per-driver bill/admin view into launch scope, or record the support gap as an accepted risk with an interim manual path.
- `payment_events.idempotency_key` is varchar(64) while the convention allows 255 — new deterministic keys must fit 64 chars or the column widens in the same migration; purchase response-shape changes must be additive fields so old idempotency replays stay interpretable.
- Governance preconditions: a dated recorded amendment to the Zero Escrow ruling text ("drivers pay the platform only for lead subscription packs via PortPos" → postpaid credit keeps "never holds rider trip money" true but changes the payment-direction sentence — future agents will otherwise classify postpaid bills as a ruling violation); AGENTS.md write-ownership additions; a Copy Truth inventory for every new bill/lockout string at ship time.

## Goals vs. Non-Goals

* **In-Scope Goals:**
  - Per-driver product possession (subscription package | commission package) as the monetization mode; no default; new drivers choose at onboarding; existing drivers keep current state unchanged (verified: possession gating reproduces today's populations exactly on ship day — drivers receiving offers all hold active subscriptions; drivers without receive nothing — zero backfill).
  - Fully postpaid billing for both products; subscription bills at purchase, commission bills per completed trip; one unpaid-obligations figure with a single authoritative source.
  - Settle-first purchase gate; ceiling-based dispatch lockout; manual dues payment; hard lockout on lapse with no silent conversion.
  - Mutual zeroing enforced at completion from a ride-time mode snapshot.
  - Driver-facing state machine with home-screen surfacing, one-tap recovery per state, itemized dues, and the full error-code inventory.
  - Lockout/unlock/billing-outcome observability and exposure metrics; admin locked-drivers surface.
  - Kill-switch-gated staged rollout (schema → utils-server → API/EAS) with a written rollback policy.

* **Out-of-Scope Non-Goals:**
  - Rider-side payment flows (riders still pay drivers directly; COD rules unchanged).
  - Fleet, shops, and delivery-man commission domains (already independently 0%-defaulted; courier prior art is cited for reuse-learning only, M-1).
  - Refunds, referral rewards, wallet cashback interactions with bills (deferred; each is a future bill-reduction path that MUST go through the single bills writer when built).
  - Multi-city, multi-class fare changes; the v6 engine rollout itself (only its commission input is in scope, B6).

## Alternatives Weighed

- **Unpaid obligations: dedicated bills table vs derived view over existing tables.** Converged constraint, open choice: both lenses agree the unified TOTAL must be one database-level definition (B7), but zoom_out favored a pure derived aggregate over `payment_events` + commission sums (zero new write paths) while implementation_readiness and structural showed `payment_events` cannot safely encode "owed" (B3: the orphan sweep and repair path actively mutate those rows, and the callback's `subscription_id` short-circuit blocks same-event settlement). A dedicated bills table fenced from legacy machinery, with the total expressed as a view over bills + commission, satisfies both; a payment_events-only aggregate satisfies neither. Decision carried to `/egm-draft` as the first structural choice.
- **Global revenue_model toggle: delete vs keep as kill-switch.** Converged: keep it solely as a launch/migration kill-switch with its residual semantics written as the single remaining behavior (OFF → today's subscription gating), retire the per-dispatch parameter into per-driver classification (B5), and leave deletion as later cleanup. No third state; seven test suites currently fossilize the toggle and must be updated in the same change.
- **Commission package: new product table vs a `packages` row with a type discriminator.** Converged: a `packages` row (`price_bdt = 0`, additive `product_type`, matched by `code`), purchased through the existing zero-amount rail; the −1 sentinel trap (B5) handled by branching on product type, never on call count.
- **Frozen calls (A2′): new frozen state vs natural lapse.** Open owner decision (M-4): an additive `frozen_at` column with a shared predicate is implementable but is unbudgeted machinery serving an edge case; natural lapse is simpler and consistent with hard lockout but changes what "switch" means for paid-up calls. The onboarding screen's switch-later copy depends on this.
- **Mode at dispatch: pool-level constant vs per-driver in-tx classification.** Converged: per-driver classification inside the debit transaction (B5); the pool-level global demotes to a default/kill-switch.
- **Mode at completion: live re-derivation vs ride-time snapshot.** Converged: snapshot at offer/accept (repo precedent: MFS settlement snapshot, `complete+api.ts:430-435`); completion never re-derives.

## Key Decisions & Staging for Next Steps

Six structural decisions the design document must make before any implementation brief (all consequences of the owner rulings, implementable within them):

1. **Bill representation** — dedicated bills table (recommended by the migration evidence) + unified-total view; kind-discriminated settlements from day one (B3, B7).
