# Executive recommendation

> **Brand lock:** Use **Ride** only. Do not use GoRide, GlideX or any other legacy/alternative brand name in store metadata, graphics, screenshots, campaign assets or support copy.

> **Publication rule:** The product brief is the source for intended functionality. It is not independent proof that a feature, city, payment method, provider network or emergency integration is already live. The copy below is qualified where necessary. Complete the verification gates in Section 10 before publishing stronger claims.

Position Ride as a **Bangladesh-focused everyday mobility, commerce and logistics platform** - not as a ride-hailing-only app and not as a catalogue of unrelated services. Lead with the most understandable, repeat-use customer jobs (rides, food, local shopping and delivery), then make higher-intent specialist services discoverable through screenshots, localized metadata and audience-specific store listings.

**Recommended starting Google Play title:** `Ride: Rides, Food & Delivery`  
**Recommended starting short description:** `Rides, food, groceries, rentals and parcel delivery in supported areas.`

This is a starting hypothesis, not a proven ranking winner. Validate it with Play Console acquisition and store-listing data after launch.

# 1. Core positioning and jobs-to-be-done

## 1.1 One-sentence value proposition

**Ride brings everyday mobility, local commerce and delivery into one Bangladesh-focused app - helping people arrange rides, rentals, shifting, ambulance transport, food, local-shop purchases and parcel delivery, with availability depending on service area and participating providers.**

## 1.2 Positioning statement

For people and businesses in Bangladesh who need to move themselves, goods or everyday purchases, Ride is a multi-service local platform that connects customers with participating drivers, fleets, restaurants, shops, workshops and delivery partners. Unlike a single-purpose booking app, Ride groups seven connected service verticals in one customer and partner ecosystem.

## 1.3 Customer personas and jobs-to-be-done

| Persona | Job to be done | Ride value to communicate | Proof the product should show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily commuter | When I need to cross town, help me find a suitable bike, scooter, CNG or car and understand the fare before I confirm. | Multiple vehicle choices; fare information; booking and trip status. | A real booking flow, vehicle catalogue, fare breakdown and live trip screen. |
| Female traveler | When I prefer a female driver, let me request the women-only matching option where it is supported. | Optional women-only matching, without implying a guaranteed match. | The actual preference control, eligibility rules, and clear no-match handling. |
| Home / office mover | When I move furniture or goods, help me request a pickup, covered van or appropriately sized truck. | Vehicle capacity choices from mini-pickup to larger cargo vehicles. | Real fleet inventory, payload guidance, quote/booking flow and coverage. |
| Medical transport user | When a patient needs transport, help me find available ambulance options and understand provider capability. | Ambulance request flow and clearly presented BLS/ALS capability where offered. | Provider credential evidence, service hours, response process and emergency escalation. |
| Food customer | When I want a meal, help me discover participating nearby restaurants and place an order. | Local restaurant discovery, order status and delivery tracking where live. | Active restaurant catalogue, checkout and genuine order tracking. |
| Shop customer | When I need daily essentials or vehicle-related products/services, help me find participating local shops or workshops. | Groceries, fresh produce, general shop products, automotive parts and workshop appointments. | Active merchants, accurate catalogues, stock/price handling and appointment availability. |
| Supply-side partner (additional) | When I want work or customers, help me join the relevant Ride service and receive eligible requests. | Driver call-package model, merchant delivery requests and provider/merchant workflows. | Role-specific onboarding, verification, package terms and partner dashboards. |

## 1.4 Message hierarchy

- **Primary consumer message:** Rides, food, local shopping and delivery in one app.
- **Secondary service discovery:** Car rental/intercity, truck and shifting, ambulance, parcel/courier, workshops and automotive products.
- **Supply-side message:** Drivers, fleets, merchants, workshops and delivery partners can participate under clearly disclosed partner terms.
- **Brand voice:** Direct, useful, local, calm and specific. Avoid exaggerated claims such as "best", "cheapest", "#1", "instant everywhere" or guaranteed response times.

## 5.4 Keyword prioritization rules

1. **Map each term to one clear intent and one relevant destination.** Do not send an ambulance searcher to a page that only showcases burgers and bikes.
2. **Begin with operationally strong services.** Search demand cannot compensate for unavailable supply or poor fulfilment.
3. **Use exact language from real users.** Collect phrases from support tickets, driver interviews, merchant onboarding and customer search logs, then remove personal information before analysis.
4. **Track outcomes beyond installs.** A keyword that attracts installs but no bookings may be a poor acquisition source.
5. **Do not repeat words to meet a density target.** Google advises against excessive length, repetition and irrelevant metadata. There is no official 3-5 repetition formula.
6. **Do not use competitor names in Apple's keyword field.** Do not use trademarks or competitor names in store metadata without authorization.
7. **Do not claim keyword volumes or traffic scores without a dated data source.** Record the tool, geography, date, volume/difficulty methodology and confidence.

# 8. Google Play Custom Store Listing plan

Use Custom Store Listings to match a user's search or campaign intent with a focused page. Google Play supports keyword-targeted custom listings using search terms and their available variations. A custom listing is not a separate app; it is an audience-specific version of the same app's store presence.

| Listing | Audience / intent | Hero headline | First screenshots | Traffic source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Default - Customers** | General consumer discovery | One app for everyday services | Overview, rides, food/shops, parcel/rentals | Organic store traffic and broad consumer campaigns |
| **Driver / ride partner** | Bike, scooter, CNG and car drivers | Keep your trip fare - see partner terms clearly | Driver onboarding, vehicle selection, lead package terms, trip workflow | Driver recruitment ads, partner referrals and driver groups |
| **Merchant / store delivery** | Retailers, F-commerce and e-commerce merchants | Delivery support for your local orders | Merchant order flow, delivery-person request, COD workflow and reconciliation | Merchant outreach and business campaigns |
| **Truck / shifting** | Home movers and cargo customers | Pickup and truck options for moving goods | Vehicle capacity, pickup/quote flow, route and contact | Moving/cargo searches and relevant campaigns |
| **Ambulance** | Medical transport intent | Find ambulance options where available | Actual provider types, request flow, safety/limitations | Only when local ambulance inventory and operational process are active |

**Recommended rollout:** Start with the Default listing plus Driver and Merchant listings. Add Truck and Ambulance listings only when there is sufficient operational coverage and a distinct acquisition audience. Do not create thin listings that lead to unavailable services.

**Driver message guardrail:** Say "no per-trip commission" and explain that weekly call packages are required to access eligible ride leads. Never imply that drivers pay nothing or keep all gross earnings without any package costs.

# 9. Measurement and experiment plan

## 9.1 What to measure

| Funnel stage | Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Store discovery | Store listing visitors / impressions and traffic source | Understand where qualified users originate. |
| Store intent | Unique install clicks and listing conversion | Measures the store page's direct contribution. |
| Activation | First completed ride, first delivered food/grocery order, first parcel delivered | Separates installs from meaningful use. |
| Supply | Verified and active drivers, fleet providers, merchants and delivery partners | Measures marketplace readiness. |
| Retention | Day-1 and Day-7 returning users by role and vertical | Detects whether acquired users find recurring value. |
| Quality | Crashes, ANRs, failed bookings, cancellations, support contacts | Prevents growth experiments from masking product problems. |
| Economics | Cost per activated user, first transaction and active partner | Connects acquisition to business performance. |

Keep cohorts separate by service and persona. A rider install, a driver install and a merchant install should not be treated as equivalent activations.

## 9.2 Google Play listing experiments

Current Play Console documentation states that an app can run **one default graphics experiment** or **up to five localized experiments** at a time. Default graphics experiments can test the icon, feature graphic and screenshots. Localized experiments can also test descriptions. Play Console's target metrics include **Unique user install clicks**, **Unique user open clicks** and **Unique user pre-registration clicks**. The Console estimates the time and volume needed for a statistically significant result.

### Suggested sequence

| Window | Test | One main variable | Primary metric | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | No test; establish current listing data | None | Unique install clicks / listing performance | Record baseline by country, language and acquisition source. |
| Test 1 | App icon | Icon concept A vs B | Unique user install clicks | Use Console's significance estimate; do not force a 1,000-installs-per-variant rule. |
| Test 2 | Screenshot 1 | Super-app overview vs mobility-first message | Unique user install clicks | Keep other assets stable during the test. |
| Test 3 | Bengali localization | Native Bangla copy/UI assets vs current Bangla treatment | Localized listing install clicks | Run only after native-language and UI review. |
| Test 4 | Service-specific listing | Merchant/driver/ambulance message, on separate listings | Listing-level acquisition and downstream activation | Compare downstream activation separately; Play experiment target metrics are not a retained-installer metric. |

Allow tests to run long enough to cover weekday and weekend behavior and until Console results are useful. Do not change several assets in the same experiment and then attribute the outcome to one element. Google does not publish a universal 1,000-installer or 90% confidence rule for all Play experiments.

## 9.3 Apple product page optimization

App Store Connect Product Page Optimization can test up to three alternate product-page treatments against the original for supported assets such as icons, screenshots and app previews. Use its built-in analytics and confidence indicators. Apple Custom Product Pages can provide distinct visual/promo experiences through unique URLs for relevant campaign audiences.

## 9.4 Review and rating policy

- Ask for feedback after a completed core action, but do not ask for a five-star rating or gate the native review prompt based on a satisfaction answer.
- Do not buy, incentivize, fabricate or selectively solicit positive reviews.
- Reply to reviews to resolve the actual issue. Do not write keyword-stuffed replies.
- Use private support channels to handle problems, but do not make access to support conditional on not reviewing publicly.

## 10.1 Google Play technical and disclosure gates

- **Location:** Request the minimum location scope needed. If the app uses background location, Google requires the use to be core to the app, a declaration process, a demonstration video and a prominent in-app disclosure; the privacy policy and store listing disclosures must also be consistent.
- **App access:** Give reviewers working access to role-restricted customer, driver, merchant and provider flows. Provide test credentials and instructions for OTP, location, payment sandbox, booking and emergency flows. Do not require the reviewer to have a real emergency or a real provider to use the app.
- **Data safety / privacy:** Inventory data collected by the app and all included SDKs. Ensure the Data safety form, privacy policy, in-app disclosures and actual backend behavior agree.
- **Account deletion:** If users can create accounts, confirm the required in-app deletion and external deletion request path are implemented and operational.
- **Payments:** Google Play's policy distinguishes physical goods/services from digital app functionality. Ride's ride fares, food delivery and physical rentals are different from a package that may unlock digital access to ride leads. The weekly driver call package must receive a specific Payments-policy review before release; do not assume it is exempt just because a ride is eventually a physical service.
- **Pricing disclosure:** Clearly show package price, lead allowance, validity, rollover, renewal and cancellation/refund terms before a driver purchases a package. Listing copy must not say the service is free if package access requires payment.
- **Emergency safety:** Do not represent Ride as a substitute for 999 or emergency medical response. Test all SOS behavior, fallback behavior, network failure states and user disclosures.
